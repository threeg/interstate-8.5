## 1. Why this version exists

Slice 1's real job was a **viability test**: could Claude Design produce a good enough look to break the
design stall that killed v3 and v4? It answered yes. But nine milestones of building against that answer
taught us things the slice-1 specification could not have known, and two of them are now parked decisions
(`TODO-001`, `TODO-002`) with a third — how the design sources are *organised* — visible only in
hindsight.

Slice 2 is therefore a **consolidation slice: it adds no archive content type.** It finishes the two
screens slice 1 actually designed (the homepage and the song page), fixes the design-source structure
before that structure is copied into every future slice, closes the reproducibility gap, and clears two
repository-hygiene items that block routine maintenance — the missing root `README.md` and a Composer
setup that currently refuses ordinary module and security updates. Slice 3 opens the next content type on
a clean base.

> **It is consolidation, but it is not small — and the draft review is what changed that.** Replacing the
> homepage stub with a real Layout Builder content type (§2 B) and choosing a default-content mechanism
> (§2 C) are now the bulk of the slice, not a tidy-up around the edges. Both are still *finishing slice 1
> properly* rather than new territory, and they are one job rather than two — the homepage node is exactly
> the content the reproducibility mechanism has to carry. Naming the size honestly here beats discovering
> it at ticket generation.

**The cost of not doing this now is asymmetric.** Every one of the six scope items gets more expensive
with each slice added on top: a design-source layout is copied by the next section, a reproducibility gap
widens with every content entity added, a song-page composition that "does not work" becomes the pattern
later pages imitate, and a dependency set that cannot take an update accumulates unapplied security
releases for as long as it stays broken. None of them gets cheaper by waiting.

## 2. Goals

Slice 2 delivers six things. **A and B are new behaviour** (new `FR` numbers); **C and D are corrections
to slice 1's output** (largely amend-in-place); **E and F are repository hygiene** — no site-visible
change, but both block routine maintenance today.

### A. Song page — alternate-version composition (resolves `TODO-001`)

`INT8-020` shipped `FR-13`/`FR-20` as specified and its 17 Playwright assertions pass, but the result was
judged unsatisfying on review. The hi-fi only ever draws the lyric-pair **as an isolated panel**, never
inside the full song-page layout, so *where it sits in the page* was never actually decided by the design.

Slice 2 makes that decision and rebuilds to it, resolving the three things `TODO-001` names: where the
pair sits relative to the quote, lyrics, notes and right-hand rail; whether it keeps the panel framing
(border + `--color-tint` header bar) or dissolves into the page; and how the parent cross-link and the
"alternate versions" list relate to it.

**Reuse, not restart.** The `lyric-pair` SDC is deliberately structural (two slots, no props), the
song-versions resolution already lives in `i8_services` (`INT8-035`), and `song-versions.spec.ts` asserts
*behaviour* (`FR-13`/`FR-20`) rather than layout — only its side-by-side/stacking checks are geometry-
specific. A redesign should need to revisit those and little else.

### B. A real homepage — Layout Builder content type, hero, and the nav over it (new)

Today the front page is a **stub**: `PageController::front()` in `i8_services` returns a single
paragraph at `/home`, routed by `i8_services.routing.yml`, with `system.site.yml` pointing `front` at
it. It was written in `INT8-017` purely so the primary nav had somewhere real to send visitors
(`FR-16`) without building the homepage, and its own docblock says so.

Slice 2 **replaces the stub with the real thing**:

- **A homepage content type with Layout Builder enabled**, and the hero block attached to it. The
  northstar brief already commits to Layout Builder "scoped narrowly" (`brief.md` §8), so this is that
  commitment landing on its first page rather than a new direction.
- **The stub goes** — controller, route, and the `system.site.yml` front-page value all move to the
  node.
- **The homepage node is content**, which is precisely why this belongs in the same slice as C. It is
  the concrete case the reproducibility mechanism has to carry.

A **second hero component**, homepage-only, sitting alongside the existing `page_hero`. **That it is a
second block type rather than an extension of `page_hero` is settled here, not left to M12** — the two
heroes are different things on the site's front door and are expected to diverge further, and a shared
type carrying an "is this the homepage?" field is the worse of the two shapes. M12 decides its fields
and placement, not its existence:

- It is a block, like the existing one, but displays a **set message** rather than a page title.
- It draws from a **library of images**, shown at random rather than fixed per page.
- The **primary nav must work over it** — transparent while the hero is in view, solid on scroll.

**Most of that already exists — reuse it, don't re-decide it.** Slice 1 built the image library and its
random behaviour in `INT8-028`, and both are **settled decisions this brief does not reopen**:

- **The library is a field, and its modelling is fixed.** `field_background_images` on
  `block_content.type.page_hero` is a cardinality-unlimited entity reference to `media:image`, populated
  with core's stock media-library widget. The new hero reuses that shape; M12 confirms the reuse rather
  than choosing a model.
- **The random pick is solved, including the cache trap.** `HeroBackgroundFormatter` renders **one
  deterministic, fully-cacheable candidate server-side and hands the whole candidate set to the browser,
  which rerolls on every page load.** That is deliberate: Drupal's internal page cache stores anonymous
  responses *permanently* regardless of render-array max-age, and BigPipe is a no-op for sessionless
  anonymous requests, so a server-side pick would freeze for every anonymous visitor. The formatter is a
  *field* formatter and knows nothing about being in a block, so it should carry across unchanged.
  **What is actually owed is a requirement, not a decision:** this behaviour is specified nowhere — it
  lives in a PHP docblock and a field description. M11 writes it down as an `FR`.

**The nav half is smaller than it looks.** `site-header.js` already implements exactly this: a
`site-header--transparent` variant that toggles `is-scrolled` past 24px, with
`--color-nav-hover-on-transparent` already in the token set for the hover state it needs. Slice 2's work
there is to **wire the new hero to the existing variant and prove it** with a front-end test, not to
build the behaviour. The requirements delta should say so, so a ticket is not written for work already
done.

**What already tests the front page.** `front-page-nav.spec.ts` and `page-shell.spec.ts` assert the
rendered front page today. Swapping the stub for a node changes what backs those assertions but not what
they are asserting, so they are the natural place to prove the swap — and, once C lands, the check that a
freshly installed site actually has a homepage.

**Where the line sits.** Slice 2 builds the homepage's **shell and its first block** — the content type,
its Layout Builder configuration, and the hero. It does **not** build the homepage's remaining sections
(§5): no news row, no contribute panel, no discography strip. Those need content types that do not exist
yet, which is what slice 3+ is for. The test is whether a section needs a content type slice 2 is not
building — if it does, it is out.

**One knock-on to settle — smaller than it first looked.** The existing `page_hero` is placed as a
**region block** (`block.block.interstate_85_pageherobackground.yml`), and placing the homepage hero
**inside a Layout Builder layout** is a different mechanism, so M12 owes a decision on whether the two
heroes share a placement model or deliberately diverge. What it does **not** owe is *what stops the
region block rendering on the homepage as well*: that block already carries
`visibility.request_path` with `negate: true, pages: '<front>'`, so it is excluded from the front page
today. M12's job there is to confirm that exclusion still holds once `<front>` resolves to a node
rather than the stub route — not to invent the mechanism.

### C. Install-required content is reproducible (resolves `TODO-002`)

Three pieces of the shipped site live only in the development database: the primary nav's **Home** and
**Songs** links (the Songs one being the concrete realisation of `FR-16`), the five footer labels, and
the `page_hero` background block — whose UUID `block.block.interstate_85_pageherobackground.yml`
hard-references in both `dependencies.content` and its `plugin` key. A fresh `site-install` +
`config:import` produces a site with no primary navigation, an empty footer row and a broken hero block,
**while every gate stays green**, because `lando test` and `lando playwright` both run against the
existing database.

`TODO-002` left two things open. **Goal B settles the second one:** the homepage node, its Layout Builder
layout, the hero block and its image library are all *more* install-required content, on the same footing
as the three entities already missing — and unlike them, the homepage is the site's front door. Slice 2
cannot ship B and leave the mechanism undecided, so "is it in scope at all" is now answered: **yes.**

That leaves **which mechanism**, and M12 owes that decision. Two constraints shape it:

- **Seed, don't enforce.** The requirement is a default content set that spins up on install and is then
  **freely editable at whatever it is deployed onto** — not an artefact re-imported over the operator's
  edits. That rules out treating content the way `NFR-6` treats configuration, and it is the sharper
  version of what `TODO-002` was asking.
- **Survey contrib before building anything.** Lazy adoption says don't adopt without a trigger; the
  trigger now exists, so the answer is a module rather than an owned `hook_install()` unless the survey
  says otherwise. Candidates for M12 to evaluate — **and to confirm are Drupal 11-ready**, which this
  brief does not assert — include `default_content`, `default_content_deploy`, `single_content_sync`, and
  `structure_sync` for the menu links specifically. Hand-rolling is the fallback, not the starting point.

### D. Design sources restructured (new — process correction)

Slice 1 put all three screens, the component library and the token panel into a **single Claude Design
page**, exported as one file (`Interstate-8 1B.dc.html`). The **authority rules** written around it
(`design-system.md` §1.1) are sound and stay; the **file layout** does not scale and is replaced:

1. **One design page per major page/section**, exported one file per page — `song-page.dc.html`,
   `release-landing.dc.html` and so on — each the sole source of truth for the page it names.
2. **A dedicated tokens page** and **a dedicated components page**, so a component's shape is defined
   once rather than redrawn inside every page that uses it.
3. **Wireframes feed design; design feeds code.** Wires are a quick, low-fidelity way to agree
   *placement*; implementation is built from the design file only, never from a wire.

**Why one file per page is the right call.** Four reasons, in order of weight for this project:

- **Slices add pages.** Under the current layout, slice 3 must edit the one file every prior slice
  depends on. Under the new one it adds a file. That is the difference between a design source that
  scales with the delivery model and one that fights it.
- **Reviewable diffs.** A change to the song page currently rewrites the file the songs landing also
  binds to, so no diff can show which page changed.
- **Freezing.** A shipped page's design cannot be held stable while another iterates when both live in
  one artefact.
- **Citable authority.** A ticket's `## Design authority` currently cites one file plus a screen name;
  a reader cannot verify they read the same region twice. A filename is checkable.

**The risk this introduces, and the rule that contains it.** Standalone page files duplicate components,
and duplicated components drift. The components page is what prevents that, but only with an explicit
precedence rule: **the components page is authoritative for a component's shape and states; a page file
is authoritative for composition and placement only.** Without that, the same button exists in six files
in six shapes and nothing says which is right. Milestone 14 must write this rule, not just produce the
files.

### E. The repository has a root `README.md` (new — repository hygiene)

There is no `README.md` at the repository root. What exists is `CLAUDE.md` (standing instructions for the
agent) and `spec/README.md` (the method). Neither is the document a human arriving at the repository
needs, and neither is what a code host renders on the landing page.

The gap is small but real: **the fastest way to stand this project up is currently to read an agent's
instruction file.** `CLAUDE.md`'s *Commands* section holds the `lando` invocations, `spec/README.md` holds
the method, and the migration and test procedures are spread across `tooling/` scripts and
`spec/test-strategy/test-strategy.md` — with nothing pointing at any of it.

Scope is deliberately narrow: **an orientation document, not a second specification.** What the project
is, the stack in a sentence, how to stand it up locally, how to run the gates, where the spec lives, and
the licence. It **links** to the authoritative documents rather than restating them — a README that
duplicates `CLAUDE.md` is a README that will contradict it inside one version. Goal F is what makes the
"stand it up" section true rather than aspirational, which is why the two are one slice.

### F. Composer dependency management is standardised (new — repository hygiene)

The project is meant to be a `drupal/recommended-project` layout, and structurally it is: the scaffold
configuration, `installer-paths`, and the `drupal/drupal` conflict all match the upstream template, with
deliberate local additions for custom modules, custom themes and `recipes/`. But **routine module and
security updates currently fail with version-lock errors**, which is a maintenance problem, not a
cosmetic one — the PHP-EOL trap is what ended v2, and an unupdatable dependency set is the same failure
mode one level up.

Slice 2 diagnoses and standardises this. **Three concrete divergences are already visible and are the
starting point, not the conclusion** — M17 confirms each against the real error before changing anything:

- **`drupal/core-recommended` pins 45 transitive dependencies.** It is required at `^11` but resolves to
  a package whose own `require` block constrains `drupal/core` to exactly `11.4.2` and pins
  `symfony/*`, `guzzlehttp/*`, `doctrine/*` and forty-odd others to `~` ranges. That is what the package
  is *for* — reproducible core installs — but it is also the textbook cause of "cannot be resolved /
  locked to version" when a contrib module wants a newer transitive dependency. **The standard trade is
  `drupal/core-recommended` → `drupal/core`**, buying updatability at the cost of guaranteed-tested
  transitive versions. That trade is the decision, and it should be made deliberately rather than
  inherited.
- **There is no `config.platform.php`, and the container runs PHP 8.3.** Without a platform key, Composer
  resolves against whichever PHP ran the command. Run on the host, that is not necessarily the
  appserver's 8.3 — so resolution can differ between machines and produce a set that installs on one and
  breaks on the other. Pinning `platform.php` to the Lando appserver's version makes the resolution
  reproducible wherever it runs.
- **`minimum-stability` is `dev`.** Widening stability broadens the candidate set for every package, and
  it is looser than the upstream template. M17 confirms whether anything in the requirement set actually
  needs it; if nothing does, it tightens.

> **The cause is assumed, not verified — see `S-2`.** The failing command and its error text have not been
> seen; the three divergences above were read off `composer.json` and `composer.lock` and are *consistent
> with* the symptom rather than proven to cause it. `S-2` in `spec/open-questions.md` carries that
> assumption so it is swept rather than trusted. **M17 reproduces the real failure first**, and if the cause
> turns out to be something else, this section's scope is what changes.

**What "standardised" has to mean, or this recurs.** Not just a working `composer update` once, but the
procedure written down where a human will find it (goal E's README) and the constraint recorded as a
requirement — so a later slice cannot quietly re-pin its way back into the same corner.

**A recommendation on D-e and D-f, for the milestone to accept or reject.** `design-system.md` §1.1

## 4. Success criteria

Slice 2 is done when:

1. The song page's alternate-version view matches a design that draws it **inside the real page**, and
   `song-versions.spec.ts` still asserts `FR-13`/`FR-20` behaviour.
2. The front page is a **Layout Builder node**, not a controller: `PageController::front()`, the
   `i8_services.front` route and the `/home` front-page value are gone, and `FR-16`'s nav link still
   resolves.
3. That homepage renders the new hero with **its set message** — the part that is actually new — over an
   image drawn from the library by the existing `INT8-028` mechanism, with the primary nav transparent
   over it and solid on scroll. Covered by a Playwright test, and the per-page-load reroll is by then a
   written `FR` rather than an undocumented behaviour.
4. A fresh `site-install` + config import produces a site with working primary navigation, the footer
   labels, an intact hero block **and the homepage node with its layout** — by the mechanism M12 selects
   — and that content is **editable afterwards without being overwritten**. `TODO-002` is tombstoned.
5. `spec/design/` holds one design file per page, a tokens page and a components page, with
   `design-system.md` §1 and §1.1 amended to match, the superseded `Interstate-8 1B.dc.html` marked as
   such rather than deleted (existing tickets cite it), and root `CLAUDE.md`'s *Where things live* updated.
6. A **root `README.md`** exists: what the project is, the stack, standing it up locally, running the
   gates, where the spec lives, the licence — **linking** to the authoritative documents rather than
   restating them.
7. **A contrib module can be updated and a security release applied** without a dependency-resolution
   failure — demonstrated, not asserted — and the procedure is in the README. Composer resolution is
   reproducible regardless of which PHP runs the command.
8. `lando test` and `lando playwright` both green; `INT8-047` (reconcile the `INT8-043`–`046` record
   drifts) closed.

## 5. Out of scope

- **The homepage's remaining sections** — no news row, no contribute panel, no discography strip. The
  homepage **content type, its Layout Builder configuration and the hero are in scope**; what those
  sections would *display* is not, because each needs a content type slice 2 is not building. That is the
  test: if a section depends on Releases, Concerts/Setlists or News, it is out.
- **The archive content types** — Releases, Concerts/Setlists and News all stay for slice 3+. The
  homepage type is the sole exception, and it is a page-shell type, not archive data.
- **Cross-entity relationships on songs** — the seams stay nullable and unpopulated.
- **Search API / faceted search** — still deferred, still no trigger.
- **setlist.fm integration** — still deferred.
- **Re-doing slice 1's designs wholesale.** D is a *restructure* of the sources plus the two pages named
  in A and B. Existing screens are migrated into the new layout as they are, not redrawn.
- **A dependency-upgrade sweep.** Goal F makes updates *possible* and proves it on one module plus a
  security release. It is **not** a mandate to take every available update — a broad version bump is its
  own risk with its own testing, and bundling it here would hide whether the standardisation itself
  worked.
- **A README that documents the architecture, the method or the requirements.** Goal E is an orientation
  page that links to `spec/`. Anything that would duplicate a `spec/` document belongs in that document.
